🎯 Why your grades matter

Whatever you end up doing, building strong habits now keeps every future option open.

✅
Foundation — every door stays open
Keeping English and Maths strong (grade 4+ equivalent) is the baseline for almost every route after school.
📚
Solid — sixth form and college routes
Aiming for grade 6+ in Maths and Science keeps sixth form and A-level options open. Grade 4+ across most subjects opens college and Level 3 apprenticeships.
🚀
Strong — competitive opportunities
A broad spread of subjects at grade 5+ opens competitive university courses and premium apprenticeship schemes later on.
⭐
High-demand routes — worth knowing early
Medicine, Dentistry, Veterinary Science — these need very high grades and Science/Maths at grade 6+ minimum for A-level entry. Good to be aware of this early, no pressure yet.

🏔 Getting Ready for DofE
You can start Bronze Duke of Edinburgh from age 14 — usually Year 9 or 10. Use this space to get ready and plan ahead.

📚 About the award: The Duke of Edinburgh Award has three levels — Bronze, Silver and Gold. Each involves four sections: Volunteering, Physical, Skill and Expedition. Gold also includes a Residential. You don't need to start yet — but building habits now (a hobby, some volunteering, staying active) makes Bronze much easier when you're old enough to sign up.

⚠️ A couple of things worth knowing early

No pressure at all — just useful to have in the back of your mind.

🩺 Medicine, Dentistry & Vet Science
These need Chemistry and Biology at A-level later on, which means Science and Maths at grade 6+ is usually needed at GCSE first. Very competitive, but knowing this early is genuinely helpful, not a reason to worry.
🎭 Performing Arts & Music
Specialist colleges often use auditions and portfolios as much as grades. If this interests you, it's worth starting to build up examples of your work now.
✈️ Armed Forces, Trades & Apprenticeships
Lots of great routes don't need A-levels at all — apprenticeships let you earn while you learn from 16. English and Maths still matter a lot here.
🎓 Y9 Options
Choosing your GCSE subjects for Year 10 & 11. This becomes relevant from Year 9 — for now, it's just useful to know how it works.
💡 What are ‘options’?

Alongside subjects everyone studies, you'll choose some additional GCSE subjects near the end of Year 9. These run for two years (Year 10 & 11), so it's worth thinking about what genuinely interests you — not just what your friends are picking.

📚 Subjects everyone studies

Whatever you choose, these form the core of every student's timetable:

📚 English Language
📚 English Literature
🔢 Maths
🧪 Combined Science (2 GCSEs)
🌎 Geography or History
🏃 Core PE (no qualification)

Triple Science (3 separate GCSEs instead of Combined Science) is available as an option choice.

🧭 Two ways of choosing

Kimberley offers two options pathways. The English Baccalaureate pathway includes a Modern Foreign Language and is recommended for students aiming for the full EBacc (a measure that includes English, Maths, two sciences, History or Geography, and a language, all at grade 5+). The Main pathway gives more flexibility across the full range of subjects. The school will guide you towards whichever fits you best — there's no need to decide this alone.

💕 Wellbeing
School can be a lot sometimes. These are here whenever you need them.
💕 Feeling overwhelmed? You're not alone.

Everyone has times when things feel too much. Reaching out is a sign of strength. The links below are trusted, free, and confidential.

🧡 YoungMinds
Mental health support for young people. Advice, crisis support and a helpline.
📞 Childline
Free, confidential support. Call 0800 1111 or chat online, any time.
💬 Kooth
Free NHS-commissioned online wellbeing for young people. No waiting list.
🧠 Mind
Information and advice about mental health and where to get help.
🎓 Talk to someone at school

Your form tutor or head of year is always a good first contact. You don't need to have it all figured out before you speak to someone.

If things ever feel urgent, tell a trusted adult straightaway or call Childline on 0800 1111.

🌞 Small things that can help
💕 Talk to someone you trust
🚶 Get outside, even for 10 minutes
😴 Aim for 9–11 hours sleep
📷 Take a break from your phone each day
